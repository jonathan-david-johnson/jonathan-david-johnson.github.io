Books & Influences

The books, frameworks, and podcasts that shaped how I think about engineering management — estimation, planning, advising, people leadership, and running a firm.

Books

Software Estimation Steve McConnell · Book, 2006

Demystifying the Black Art.

The canonical reference for why software estimates go wrong and how to make them defensible. McConnell's core argument: an estimate is a probability distribution, not a single number, and most estimation failure comes from treating a target (what we want) as if it were an estimate (what's likely). Cone of uncertainty, count-compute-judge techniques, and the case for estimating in ranges instead of dates.

The Manager's Path Camille Fournier · Book, 2017

A map of every rung on the engineering ladder.

Walks the full path from new grad to CTO, with a level-headed account of what actually changes at each step — mentoring, tech leading, managing managers, executive scope. My go-to for calibrating what a role actually requires versus what a title implies, and for having language to describe the job to people who haven't done it.

Find it →
The Trusted Advisor Maister, Green, Galford · Book, 2000

Trust as a formula, not a personality trait.

Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-Orientation. The book that reframed "stakeholder management" for me as trust-building with a repeatable structure, not a soft skill. Directly useful for engineering managers who have to advise up, sideways, and into other functions without formal authority.

Frameworks

Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe) Framework

Coordinating agile teams above the team level.

A layered framework for aligning multiple agile teams around a shared program increment — portfolio, program, and team levels, with PI planning as the synchronization event. I don't run it dogmatically, but the underlying problem it solves — how independent teams stay aligned without a waterfall plan — comes up in every org past a handful of engineers.

People & Podcasts

Mitchell Hashimoto Blog

Craft notes from the builder of Terraform, Vagrant, and Ghostty.

Hashimoto writes plainly about tool design, developer experience, and the tradeoffs behind decisions most people never see the reasoning for. Consistently a good source for how to think about building things people rely on daily, at a level of detail most engineering blogs skip.

David C. Baker & the 2Bobs podcast Podcast, co-hosted with Blair Enns

The closest thing to a professional body of knowledge for running a small firm.

Baker advises creative, marketing, and digital firms; Enns handles sales and pricing. Split of subject matter: Baker on firm size, staffing, money, and sale; Enns on selling and pricing. The recurring theme I keep coming back to — the middle is the worst place to be, structural advantages cluster at the small end and the large end — reframes a lot of "should we grow" conversations.

  • Size Matters — big-firm vs. small-firm structural advantages; growth for growth's sake is the failure mode, not growth itself. Listen →
  • Performance Bands — four bands of firm performance by revenue-per-employee, and the 11 factors that move a firm between them (confidence and value-based pricing lead). Listen →
  • The Science Behind Structuring Roles — three roles a principal can never fully delegate (directing, positioning, closing), and the order roles get delegated as a firm grows. Listen →
  • The Role of Profit in a Creative Enterprise — profit as what's left after normalized principal comp, and why most owners think they're profitable when they're subsidizing the business. Listen →
  • Building a Scalable Sales Strategy — scalable means more output without proportionally more input; borrowed audiences beat cold outreach. Listen →
  • Starting…Existing…Thriving — a three-stage model of firm maturity across utilization, positioning, hiring, and pricing. Listen →
